# ROP Project Workflow - Working Draft

**Purpose:** Show how a project moves from an opportunity to seasonal delivery, learning, and adjustment.

**Current scope:** Map the whole project simply. Detail only the farm preparation-to-MEL workflow for the first MVP. This is an untested design using synthetic data, not an operational system.

## 1. Project lifecycle at a glance

1. **Opportunity and go/no-go** - A donor issues an RFP/RFA; an unsolicited or open call appears; or ROP identifies a self-funded opportunity. Decide whether to pursue it.
2. **Design foundation** - Select potential regions and target beneficiaries; use value-chain and net-income analyses to identify suitable crops and meaningful value-add activities. Define farmer selection criteria and the development logic: need/problem -> activity -> output -> intended outcome.
3. **Proposal and scope** - Develop the proposal and agree the scope, shaped by donor requirements where relevant.
4. **Funding and mobilization** - After funding or an internal go-ahead, hire staff, obtain local approvals, set up offices, and localize workflows when the country or donor requires it.
5. **Prepare delivery** - Train trainers when new trainers are needed. Recruit and select farmers using the design-stage criteria. Collect the agreed farmer contribution after selection and before official program entry or farmer training. Complete the baseline survey after enrollment as soon as MEL capacity allows; aim for before the first training, but it is not a gate.
6. **Seasonal delivery** - Plan and deliver crop-stage training; support farmers to prepare land; verify readiness; distribute inputs to farmers who pass; support planting and subsequent seasonal activities.
7. **MEL, management action, and next season** - MEL collects standardized evidence and analyzes crop, extension, and training performance, successes, problems, and follow-up needs. Project management reviews findings, assigns adjustments, owners, and due dates, then uses the learning to plan the next seasonal cycle.
8. **Closeout or transition** - Preserve final evidence, results, obligations, and lessons for the next project or phase. Details remain to be designed.

## 2. MVP workflow: farm preparation to MEL follow-up

| Step | What happens | Owner / decision | Record or result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Plan the season | Set crop-stage training, field activity dates, responsibilities, input plan, budget links, and MEL questions against the project's development logic. | Project team; exact roles follow that project's org chart. | Seasonal plan with linked activities, budget lines, owners, dates, and MEL questions. |
| 2. Train farmers | Deliver classroom and field training for the relevant crop stage (for example, land preparation and planting). Farmers must have joined officially and paid their agreed contribution before program training. | Extension agents lead technical delivery. | Training record, attendance/source evidence, issues, and follow-up actions. |
| 3. Prepare land | Farmers prepare the agreed plot to the technical specifications. | Farmer, supported by the Extension agent. | Plot linked to farmer, crop, season, and project. |
| 4. Verify readiness | Extension agent and MEL staff visit the plot and record a simple **Yes / No** finding. | Joint site visit; readiness is a yes/no decision. | Standardized field-visit form and evidence reference. |
| 5a. If Yes | Confirm readiness and distribute the farm input package. | Authorized project staff record the distribution. | Input package, quantities, date, recipient, and expenditure evidence linked together. |
| 5b. If No | Give the farmer a defined opportunity and deadline to fix the issue, then recheck. If unresolved by the deadline, remove the farmer from the project. This is expected to be rare after contribution. | Extension agent communicates corrective action; project authority confirms removal per local rules. | Issue, correction deadline, recheck result, and decision history. |
| 6. Plant and follow up | Farmer plants the targeted crop at the right time and in the right manner. Technical and MEL teams conduct follow-up site visits and complete the relevant standardized forms. | Farmer; Extension agent; MEL team. | Planting and field-visit records, including season-specific measures such as harvest data when due. |
| 7. Analyze and act | MEL analyzes what is working, what problems need action, and where follow-up is needed; organize site-visit findings for management review. | MEL prepares and validates analysis. Management reviews, sets adjustments, and assigns owners and deadlines; exact roles follow the project org chart. | Analysis linked to source records, management decisions, action owners, due dates, and next-season changes. |

**Loop:** Management actions and MEL learning feed into the next seasonal plan. Do not create a new report or task unless it replaces an existing one or has a clear user and decision purpose.

## 3. Shared information and evidence rules

- Reuse the same project, farmer, plot, crop, season, activity, budget, and evidence references across steps. Avoid copying the same facts into separate workflow-specific records.
- Keep a trace from the development need/problem to activities, outputs, outcomes, evidence, and management decisions. Also let operational records create forward actions, calendar dates, and follow-ups.
- Paper and offline records are valid source evidence. Record who completed them, when, where the original is stored, and link a digital reference when available.
- AI may help organize records, check completeness, compare results, and draft analysis. MEL staff validate evidence and interpretation; management makes decisions.
- For every new report, form, task, or automated output, record what it replaces (or why it is genuinely new) and compare effort, quality, or time saved. Remove unnecessary duplicate outputs and follow-ups.

## 4. MVP boundary and current status

- **In the MVP:** a synthetic example of the seasonal workflow above, the yes/no readiness gate, correction and recheck path, input-to-expenditure evidence links, MEL findings, and management actions.
- **Mapped only at high level:** opportunity selection, proposal, mobilization, recruitment, other seasonal activities, and closeout.
- **Not yet decided:** detailed opportunity go/no-go criteria; the baseline survey schedule and exception handling; exact project roles and approval thresholds; the current reports/forms/tasks the MVP should replace; and the minimum evidence needed in a management package.
- **Not operational:** no live farmer information, integrations, automated decisions, or real project deployment in this prototype.

## 5. Immediate next step

Run one fictional farmer/plot through steps 1-7. Note only the missing information needed to answer: **Is this farmer ready for inputs? What happened? What needs action, who owns it, and by when?** Use those gaps to shape the untested MVP.
